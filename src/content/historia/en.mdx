## Deep roots

Día de Muertos originates from Mesoamerican peoples — Aztec, Maya, Mixtec, and Zapotec — who saw death as another stage of the life cycle. They honored the dead with offerings, incense, and flowers.

## A living tradition

With Christianity, Indigenous dates merged with the Catholic calendar: November 1 for children and November 2 for adults. Over centuries, the celebration filled with color, music, and flavors that continue to evolve today.

## World heritage

In 2008, UNESCO declared this festivity an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, recognizing its value as a bridge between generations and an expression of Mexican identity.
